# HOQS North America Website Brief for Base44

## Project

Build the new home website for HOQS North America.

This site is for HOQS North America leadership, including `HermetechsGreatWork@gmail.com`, and should become the new primary public-facing website for the North American branch.

Base44 should treat this as a close-sibling adaptation of the existing HOQS Australia site, not a brand-new unrelated concept.

## Primary Template Reference

Use the HOQS Australia website as the structural and stylistic template:

- https://www.hoqs.com.au/

The North America website should be very similar because it is still HOQS. The goal is not to reinvent the brand. The goal is to localize and tune it for the American audience while preserving the HOQS identity.

## Core Instruction

This website should feel like:

- HOQS Australia, but adapted for North America
- recognizably part of the same HOQS family
- similar in layout logic, navigation, priorities, and content structure
- updated with American terminology, measurements, and tone
- slightly more friendly, upbeat, and retro in vibe
- informed by shared HOQS knowledge, community experience, and the broader PARAFLEX ecosystem

Do not turn it into a generic startup website.

Do not over-modernize it into something sleek but soulless.

Do not make it feel like a separate brand from HOQS Australia.

Do not make it feel cold, gatekept, or unfriendly to newcomers.

## Truth Guardrails

Do not invent:

- technical specs that are not confirmed
- founder claims or historical claims that are not confirmed
- product availability, shipping promises, or lead times that are not confirmed
- builder partnerships or referral relationships that are not confirmed
- manufacturing capabilities that HOQS North America does not actually provide

If exact facts are missing, use clear placeholders or neutral wording that can be replaced later.

The site should sound informed and confident, but it should not present guesses as facts.

## What To Mirror From HOQS Australia

Keep the Australian site's overall logic and simplicity:

- straightforward homepage structure
- practical, product-first navigation
- clear emphasis on PARAFLEX
- direct links to plans, CNC files, projects, drivers, builders, and contact
- community-rooted identity
- functional trust and product clarity over marketing fluff

The Australia homepage currently centers around:

- PARAFLEX speakers
- HOQS drivers
- plans
- CNC files
- flat-packs and built cabinets
- community history and founder lineage

That general framework should remain intact.

## What HOQS North America Is

HOQS North America should be presented as:

- the North American home for HOQS / PARAFLEX-related knowledge and access
- a place for plans, drivers, DXF / CNC requests, builder referrals, and project guidance
- a community-supported ecosystem with volunteers helping with testing, prototyping, validation, and development support
- a welcoming entry point for both serious builders and newcomers

HOQS North America should not be presented as:

- a direct cabinet manufacturer
- a company that builds cabinets in-house
- a generic ecommerce storefront detached from the community

## North America Adaptation

Adjust the site for American users:

- use American wording where appropriate
- use U.S. measurements and conventions
- make the tone warmer and more approachable
- introduce a more upbeat, friendly, slightly retro energy
- keep the site clear, confident, and useful

The retro direction should be subtle.

Think:

- warm industrial confidence
- classic audio culture references in tone and styling
- approachable enthusiast energy
- not kitschy
- not cartoonish
- not fake vintage

## Brand Personality

The site should feel:

- confident
- knowledgeable
- welcoming
- grounded
- community-connected
- builder-friendly
- audio-native

The tone should feel more human and inviting than the Australia site, but still informed, capable, and serious.

## Knowledge Direction

Base44 is allowed to build on shared knowledge from:

- the HOQS Australia website
- HOQS / PARAFLEX global community context
- our own project knowledge and North America-specific understanding
- the builder, volunteer, testing, and prototype culture around the ecosystem

This should be used to strengthen the site's clarity, usefulness, and authenticity.

Do not copy the brief or source material verbatim.

Do synthesize what is already known into a coherent North America version that feels informed, practical, and welcoming.

## Color Direction

Base44 should use the HOQS Australia site as the real color anchor.

The live Australia site appears to be built around a dark navy and charcoal base, not a teal-first palette.

Use these Australia-reference colors as the starting point:

- Australia primary dark: `#02133E` | `rgb(2, 19, 62)` | `hsl(223, 94%, 13%)`
- Australia charcoal: `#1C1C1C` | `rgb(28, 28, 28)` | `hsl(0, 0%, 11%)`
- Australia light background: `#F9F9F9` | `rgb(249, 249, 249)` | `hsl(0, 0%, 98%)`
- Australia support gray: `#B7B7B7` | `rgb(183, 183, 183)` | `hsl(0, 0%, 72%)`
- Australia black: `#000000` | `rgb(0, 0, 0)` | `hsl(0, 0%, 0%)`

For HOQS North America, keep that same foundation but warm it slightly.

Use this North America palette:

- Primary dark navy: `#02133E` | `rgb(2, 19, 62)` | `hsl(223, 94%, 13%)`
- Main charcoal text: `#1C1C1C` | `rgb(28, 28, 28)` | `hsl(0, 0%, 11%)`
- Warm off-white background: `#F5F1E8` | `rgb(245, 241, 232)` | `hsl(42, 39%, 94%)`
- Soft warm sand background: `#E4D6C3` | `rgb(228, 214, 195)` | `hsl(35, 38%, 83%)`
- Muted warm gray: `#8E877D` | `rgb(142, 135, 125)` | `hsl(35, 7%, 52%)`
- Warm steel blue accent: `#355C7D` | `rgb(53, 92, 125)` | `hsl(208, 40%, 35%)`
- Retro rust accent: `#A85A3A` | `rgb(168, 90, 58)` | `hsl(17, 49%, 44%)`

Color usage rules:

- use `#02133E` as the main brand anchor
- use `#1C1C1C` for most body text and dark UI structure
- use `#F5F1E8` as the main page background instead of stark white
- use `#E4D6C3` for section contrast, cards, or soft background blocks
- use `#355C7D` sparingly for restrained accent moments
- use `#A85A3A` only for small highlights, badges, hover details, or subtle retro notes

Do not:

- replace the dark HOQS identity with bright teal branding
- make the whole site beige or sepia
- overuse the rust accent
- drift into a vintage poster aesthetic

The visual result should still feel unmistakably HOQS, just a little warmer, friendlier, and more North America-specific.
